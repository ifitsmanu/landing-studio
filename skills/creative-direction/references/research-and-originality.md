# Research and originality protocol

## Research coverage

Use at least three distinct domains. A domain is a visual practice with its own constraints, not a
different website inside the same industry.

| Domain | Useful questions |
|---|---|
| Product and operating environment | What real objects, data, gestures, or workflows are native to the product? |
| Editorial and publishing | How are argument, pacing, annotation, and hierarchy made legible? |
| Packaging, identity, and wayfinding | How do few elements create recognition and guide action? |
| Industrial design and architecture | How do material, structure, scale, and tolerances communicate trust? |
| Film, title, exhibition, and stage | How are reveal, sequence, focus, and atmosphere controlled? |
| Scientific and information design | How is evidence compressed without losing precision? |
| Games and interactive systems | How does feedback teach behavior without explanatory copy? |
| Software category | What conventions aid comprehension, and which have become empty defaults? |

Do not count generated image grids, unattributed aggregators, or search-result thumbnails as research
evidence. Follow a result to the original creator or publisher and record the canonical source.

## Reference log fields

- canonical URL;
- creator or organization;
- work title and date when available;
- accessed-at date;
- domain;
- observed principle;
- product-specific application;
- distinctive expression that is out of bounds;
- rights note: `REFERENCE_ONLY` unless separately cleared by `media-sourcing`.

## Category-median fingerprint

Describe the median using observable decisions:

- hero geometry and content order;
- headline register and type treatment;
- dominant palette and surface effects;
- product proof format;
- illustration, screenshot, or photography conventions;
- CTA placement;
- interaction and entrance-motion pattern;
- common credibility gaps.

The audit is weak if it says only "generic," "clean," or "SaaS-like."

## Originality matrix

Score each territory `0`, `1`, or `2` on the following axes:

| Axis | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product specificity | Could fit any competitor | Some product cues | Idea depends on this product's truth |
| Category distance | Median reproduction | Familiar with a twist | Clearly differentiated and still legible |
| Source transformation | Recognizable borrowing | Multiple sources synthesized | Abstract principles transformed into a new system |
| Structural difference | Cosmetic variant | One meaningful shift | Governing idea and at least three execution axes differ |
| Proof integration | Decoration displaces proof | Proof is adjacent | Proof drives the composition |
| Restraint | Novelty impairs use | Manageable risk | Distinctiveness improves comprehension |

Fail and replace a territory when any of these is true:

- product specificity, source transformation, or structural difference scores `0`;
- total score is below `8/12`;
- the territory cannot be explained without naming a source brand;
- the static, narrow-screen, keyboard, or reduced-motion version loses meaning;
- an unverified claim or uncleared asset is load-bearing.

Scores are decision support, not permission to ignore a direct copying risk.

## Pairwise difference test

For every pair, list concrete differences in:

1. governing idea;
2. composition;
3. proof staging;
4. typography logic;
5. color/material logic;
6. imagery;
7. motion.

The governing idea plus at least three other rows must differ. Otherwise rebuild one territory.
