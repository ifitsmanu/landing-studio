# Channel playbooks: evidence before folklore

Channels change quickly. Platform algorithms, link treatment, formats, benchmarks, and account reach
must be treated as dated observations, not permanent laws. Choose channels from the approved audience
and distribution evidence. For every consequential tactic, record either a current primary source or a
first-party result from the account being used.

## Channel decision record

Create one row per considered channel:

| Channel | Audience evidence | Account baseline | Role | Asset | CTA carriage | Current source/date | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <channel> | <where the ICP actually participates> | <reach, clicks, conversions, sample/window> | <awareness, evaluation, conversion, retention> | <native format> | <tested link/action placement> | <official docs or first-party observation> | use/test/skip |

Never select a channel because a generic launch checklist says it is mandatory. An owned channel with
measurable qualified action may deserve more investment than a larger feed with weak message match.

## Shared rules

- Reuse one approved message house, but adapt format and length to the channel. Do not copy-paste one
  asset everywhere.
- Make every hook specific, true, and fulfilled by the destination. No bait-and-switch, fake urgency,
  engagement bait, undisclosed sponsorship, vote rings, sock puppets, or planted questions.
- Use approved links and attribution parameters. Never add tracking parameters to internal navigation.
- Verify link placement, truncation, video behavior, accessibility, disclosure, and scheduling in the
  live channel before launch. Do not rely on remembered algorithm folklore.
- Use account-level baselines with dates and denominators. External benchmarks are context only, not
  targets or automatic defect thresholds.
- Route asset craft to its owner: page to landing skills, film/cutdowns to `motion-video`, and copy to
  `landing-copywriter`.

## Landing page and site

**Role:** destination and conversion surface.

Provide the campaign promise, audience, CTA, channel-specific message-match variants, and measurement
scheme before page structure and copy freeze. Verify the real page, analytics/consent behavior, social
card, form/CTA journey, and performance before traffic starts. The page is not responsible for fixing an
unqualified audience or a false upstream promise.

## Email

**Use when:** the organization has a permissioned, relevant list with known ownership and deliverability.

Define segment, sender, subject/preheader, visible promise, CTA destination, consent basis, suppression
rules, and success/guardrail metrics. Treat privacy-proxy opens as an unreliable proxy where applicable;
prefer delivered, unique clicks, qualified sessions, conversions, replies, and unsubscribe/complaint
rates with denominators. Test links, rendering, plain-text fallback, tracking, and suppression before
sending. Do not re-blast non-openers by default; justify follow-up from consent, value, and frequency.

## LinkedIn and professional feeds

**Use when:** current account data or customer research shows the audience participates there.

Choose personal, company, document, image, text, or native-video formats from account history and the
message job. Verify current official format/link rules and run small tests when link placement is
uncertain. Measure qualified profile/page visits and downstream action, not reactions alone. Disclosure
and employee-posting rules must be explicit; coordinated posts must remain authentic, not duplicated or
astroturfed.

## X and real-time feeds

**Use when:** the audience, team, and account history support a real-time conversation.

Design the first visible unit to stand alone, but do not impose a universal thread length, link position,
or media preference. Test current rendering and link behavior on the actual account. Set an owner and
response window that operations can sustain. Measure downstream qualified action and useful replies;
high impressions without message match are not success.

## YouTube and video platforms

**Use when:** the product benefits from durable demonstration, education, or search discovery and the
team can maintain the channel.

Create title, thumbnail, description, captions/transcript, chapters when helpful, and an approved CTA.
Use native aspect-ratio compositions rather than blind center crops. Judge click-through and retention
against the channel's own historical distribution, traffic source, video length, and audience. A
retention change is a diagnostic cue, not proof of a specific creative cause.

## Press, newsletters, and creators

**Use when:** there is genuine audience fit, news value, an existing relationship, or a researched
recipient list. Record why each recipient matters. Provide a sourced fact sheet, approved media, rights,
disclosures, embargo/exclusive terms where used, and a human contact. No mass-BCC, fabricated
relationships, paid placement presented as earned coverage, or unsupported superlatives. Measure
qualified coverage and downstream outcomes, not send count.

## Communities

**Use when:** promotion is allowed and the team has real standing or a contribution that meets the
community's rules. Read current rules immediately before posting, disclose affiliation, and adapt to the
venue. Skip when the only plan is extraction. No vote manipulation, sock puppets, brigading, planted
questions, or pretending to be a customer. Community response is high variance; never make one ranking
or front-page outcome the campaign plan.

## Paid amplification

Use paid media only with an approved audience, creative rights, destination, budget/loss limit,
attribution model, conversion event, privacy/consent posture, and stop/scale rules. Start with controlled
tests, verify platform reporting against first-party outcomes, and separate creative, audience, bid, and
landing-page variables. Do not buy traffic to compensate for an unproven promise or broken journey.

## Selection and test plan

For each selected channel, finalize:

1. the audience evidence and role;
2. the native asset and owner;
3. the visible hook, proof ID, CTA, and destination;
4. current format, disclosure, and link requirements with source/date;
5. account baseline and minimum useful sample;
6. primary outcome, guardrails, and stop/continue rule;
7. QA case for rendering, link, attribution, accessibility, and consent;
8. a fallback if the channel under-delivers or becomes unavailable.

The playbook is complete when every tactic is either sourced, observed in first-party data, or clearly
labeled as an experiment.
