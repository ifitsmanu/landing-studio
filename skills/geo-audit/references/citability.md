# Source quality and citation readiness

Make important project knowledge accurate, original, accessible, and worth referencing. Do not
rewrite a landing page into isolated fragments for machines. Google explicitly says generative Search
does not require content chunking or an AI-specific style.

## What makes a strong source

A strong source usually combines:

- a clear subject and claim;
- primary evidence or a transparent methodology;
- enough context and qualification to quote accurately;
- visible author/owner, date, and update path when relevant;
- accessible text plus useful images/video;
- a canonical public URL;
- a reason to exist beyond paraphrasing other pages.

These are editorial and trust properties, not guaranteed ranking factors.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer:

1. reproducible product behavior and official documentation;
2. original benchmarks with method, sample, date, and limitations;
3. approved case studies and customer testimony with permission;
4. current pricing, security, integration, and policy pages;
5. original surveys or aggregate data with defensible privacy controls;
6. transparent comparisons using current primary sources;
7. public changelogs and release notes.

Avoid commodity summaries, unsupported superlatives, scraped/generated comparison grids, invented
reviews, anonymous quotes, and numbers whose denominator or date is missing.

## Passage review

Review important passages out of context as an accuracy test:

1. Is the subject identifiable?
2. Is the statement true and traceable to the evidence ledger?
3. Is a necessary condition or caveat present?
4. Would quoting it alone materially change the meaning?
5. Does it help the intended reader make a decision?

Failure means improve the passage for clarity and integrity. It does not produce a “chunk score,”
and passing does not predict a citation.

## Useful claim shapes

Choose by evidence, not by what appears machine-friendly:

- **Definition:** what the product is, for whom, and the actual mechanism.
- **Measured result:** value, unit, population, comparison, date, and method.
- **Boundary:** who the product is not for or when another approach is better.
- **Process:** real ordered steps with inputs and outcome.
- **Comparison:** same criteria, same date, primary sources, and honest unknowns.
- **Quote:** named, authorized person and context.
- **Original finding:** data plus methodology and limitations.

Every public fact maps to the project evidence ledger. A new claim must become an approved evidence
row before copy or markup uses it.

## Original evidence assets

Original evidence can earn references because other sources cannot reproduce it without attribution.
Good candidates include:

- a benchmark with runnable method or disclosed test conditions;
- aggregate product data published only after privacy and methodology review;
- a customer case study with approved details and quotes;
- a calculator with transparent assumptions;
- a maintained integration or capability matrix;
- a dated comparison with official sources;
- a public changelog linked to product documentation.

Publish the useful finding in accessible HTML when public discoverability is the goal. A gated
download may be a companion, but do not hide the only useful evidence behind lead capture and then
expect public retrieval.

## Comparison pages

Comparison content is high-risk and high-maintenance. Ship it only when buyers actually compare the
products and the project can maintain it.

Require:

- visible “last verified” date and owner;
- official current source per external fact;
- identical criteria applied to each product;
- `unknown` where evidence is unavailable;
- real strengths and limitations for the target product;
- no implication that a competitor endorses the comparison;
- a correction/contact path;
- scheduled review for price, availability, and capability drift.

Fairness improves buyer trust. Do not claim that conceding weaknesses mechanically causes retrieval.

## Corroboration

Map independent sources that accurately describe the product, but separate observation from action.
Earn coverage through useful work, legitimate relationships, and disclosed participation. Never buy
or seed deceptive reviews, sockpuppet discussions, undisclosed edits, or fake directory presence.

Route legitimate outreach and distribution to `marketing-campaign`. An audit does not guarantee
third-party inclusion.

## Freshness

Prioritize facts with high drift and user impact: pricing, availability, supported platforms,
integrations, limits, model names, certifications, compliance scope, locations, and policies.

Use one maintained source of truth, visible dates where meaningful, honest `dateModified`, a named
owner, and a review cadence based on actual drift. A date added only to look fresh is misleading.

## Technical availability

- Keep essential claims and links in accessible text, not only images, canvas, or video.
- Inspect the raw response and rendered DOM; consumer rendering behavior varies.
- Use semantic headings, lists, and tables because they help people and automated tools understand
  structure.
- Keep canonical URLs stable and avoid accidental walls, redirects, or bot challenges.
- Provide text alternatives, captions, and transcripts appropriate to the media.
- Verify facts and destinations after client-side state changes and localization.

## Removal triggers

Recommend removal or consolidation when a page:

- repeats another page without unique user value;
- was generated at scale to cover query variants;
- cannot be maintained accurately;
- contains fabricated, expired, or unapproved proof;
- exists only to host keyword/GEO prose;
- creates conflicting identity, price, or product facts;
- is a thin competitor page built from unsourced assumptions.

Removal is often a higher-leverage visibility improvement than adding more low-value pages.
