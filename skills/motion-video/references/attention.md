# Attention and retention

Attention is placement-specific behavior, not a universal timing formula. A website hero, paid social
ad, keynote film, YouTube video, and product demo begin under different intent, sound, viewport, and
control conditions. Use platform analytics and first-party tests when available.

## Three useful states

Design each explicitly:

1. **Poster/static state:** before playback, autoplay failure, reduced motion, share preview.
2. **Opening:** establishes the subject, tension, or product action quickly enough for the placement.
3. **Argument:** demonstrates proof, resolves the idea, and connects to the CTA.

Do not assert that a fixed percentage leaves in three seconds or reaches mid-film without data for
the actual channel. The opening still deserves disproportionate care because every viewer encounters
it and later beats cannot recover viewers who already left.

## Poster test

Export the initial/rest frame and review it at delivery size:

- one clear focal point and readable hierarchy;
- product/category recognizable when the placement requires it;
- no essential meaning dependent on animation;
- crop-safe at planned variants;
- appropriate text alternative and reduced-motion use;
- credible as a designed static asset.

## Hook candidates

Create materially different openings rather than minor word swaps:

- product already completing a meaningful action;
- a specific sourced claim;
- a real audience question answered promptly;
- a recognizable before/after state;
- a real problem state followed by the mechanism;
- a brief preview of the strongest product moment.

Logos, mood, or brand film language can be valid for a known audience or keynote context. They are
often weak for cold performance placements, but they are not universally forbidden. Select by job and
test the uncertain choice.

## Pacing

Change visual state when the story or comprehension needs it. A fixed “pattern interrupt every N
seconds” can create noise and fatigue. Vary shot length, protect reading time, and let important proof
hold long enough to understand.

For every card or scene:

1. test at final size and expected viewing distance;
2. time actual reading with representative viewers when consequential;
3. avoid simultaneous critical text and UI action unless the composition supports both;
4. give numbers their units, comparison, and qualification;
5. keep the focal point unambiguous.

Use word count and words-per-second only as early estimates. Language, typography, familiarity,
accessibility, and screen size materially change reading speed.

## Sound and muted viewing

Determine the expected sound state from the target platform and analytics. For autoplay feed or web
placements, make the core meaning survive muted playback and provide captions. For an intentional
sound-on film, sound can carry nonessential emotional and explanatory depth while captions/transcript
preserve access.

Test both passes. Do not cite an industry-wide muted percentage as the project's fact.

## Loops

For looping hero/feed assets:

- avoid a disruptive reset unless it is deliberate;
- keep the rest frame useful and compatible with the page CTA;
- prevent persistent motion from stealing reading focus;
- provide pause/reduced-motion behavior as required;
- test loop weight, battery/performance, and repeated viewing fatigue.

## Retention analysis

When trustworthy retention data exists:

1. verify the eligible audience, autoplay rules, duration, placement, and sample;
2. find meaningful changes in slope and compare variants/segments;
3. inspect the exact creative state and plausible mechanisms;
4. check whether exits reflect successful comprehension or bad friction;
5. change one causal idea when practical;
6. protect conversion and quality guardrails, not only watch time;
7. rerun long enough to distinguish novelty from a durable change.

Rewatch peaks, skips, and exits are clues, not diagnoses. Pair them with qualitative review,
click/conversion data, and the landing-page journey.
