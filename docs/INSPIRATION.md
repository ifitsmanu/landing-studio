# Inspiration research without imitation

Great creative direction does not come from asking for "a site like Linear." Use references to extract principles, then build an expression that belongs to the product, audience, and moment.

## Research lanes

| Lane | Useful sources | What to extract |
|---|---|---|
| Current web craft | [SiteInspire](https://www.siteinspire.com/), [Land-book](https://land-book.com/), [Godly](https://godly.website/), [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/) | composition, typography, interaction grammar, pacing; never assume awards imply conversion or accessibility |
| Product interaction | real target product, competitor trials, product docs, app stores, support videos | what can be demonstrated truthfully and which moment creates belief |
| Editorial systems | magazines, newspapers, books, exhibition catalogs, annual reports | hierarchy, rhythm, grids, captions, long-form trust, density |
| Physical and spatial design | packaging, wayfinding, architecture, retail, industrial design, stage design | material, scale, sequence, spatial reveal, tactility |
| Motion language | title sequences, broadcast packages, commercials, product films, music visuals | timing, transitions, shot grammar, sound relationship, restraint |
| Category culture | customer workspaces, tools, uniforms, vocabulary, rituals, communities | symbols and behaviors the audience recognizes without cliché |
| Art and visual history | museum archives, photography, printmaking, poster design, generative art | composition and technique with attribution and cultural context |

## The reference ledger

For every retained reference record:

- URL/creator/title and capture date;
- screenshot or note artifact used only for analysis;
- the specific principle worth learning;
- what does **not** transfer to this product;
- copying/trademark/cultural risks;
- the new expression proposed for this project.

Use at least three distinct lanes. If every reference is a premium SaaS homepage, the research is incomplete.

## Three-territory rule

Each territory needs a named central idea, emotional effect, proof mechanism, type/color/material logic, motion grammar, responsive behavior, performance risk, and static/reduced-motion form. At least one territory should deliberately leave the category's median visual language.

Reject cosmetic variants. "Dark," "light," and "gradient" are palettes, not ideas.

## Anti-copy test

Before approval, remove reference names from the deck and ask:

1. Does the direction still make sense from product truth alone?
2. Is any layout, phrase, illustration, interaction, or sequence recognizably derived from one source?
3. Could the same concept be pasted onto five competitors unchanged?
4. Does the concept survive without ornamental motion?
5. Can a user understand and use it on a phone, keyboard, reduced-motion mode, and weak GPU?

If any answer fails, return to creative direction before code.
